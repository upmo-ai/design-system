The chart components draw usage and trend data for pages that show how agents
are doing: runs per day, tokens by type, spend by connector. `StatTile`,
`UsageMeter`, `BarChart` and the single-series `LineChart data` form are plain
SVG and CSS with no charting dependency. The multi-series charts (`LineChart`
with `series`, `DonutChart`, `GroupedBarChart` and `ComboChart`) are built on
ECharts, loaded lazily. All of them read colour from the chart tokens only, so
they follow the active theme (light, dark and high contrast).

## Built on ECharts, loaded lazily

The four multi-series charts draw with [Apache ECharts](https://echarts.apache.org)
(`echarts`, Apache-2.0, installed with `@upmo-ai/components`). Nothing is loaded
until a chart mounts: the library arrives through a dynamic import, so a bundler
puts it in its own chunk (about 190 kB gzipped), and importing the package, a
server render, or a page that only uses `BarChart` or `LineChart data` never
fetches it. While the chunk loads, the chart holds its height with a quiet
placeholder, so nothing on the page moves. Only the pieces the charts need are
registered (line, bar and pie series, the grid, tooltip and aria modules, and the
SVG renderer), and the drawing is SVG so text stays sharp at any zoom and prints
cleanly.

Colours are read from the token variables at runtime, from the chart's own
element, so the chart follows `data-theme`, the colour scheme, `prefers-contrast`
and forced colours, and redraws when any of them changes. Every prop and data
shape is the same as before.

## Hover, tooltip and legend

- **Tooltip.** Hovering a position shows a floating tooltip with a pointer line
  (a shaded band for bars). It lists every visible series with a round swatch,
  its name and its value, largest first, through the chart's `formatValue`. A
  missing value reads "No data".
- **Legend.** Under the plot, a compact list of buttons: a round swatch and the
  series name. On the multi-series `LineChart` each item also shows the series'
  Mean and Max. Click, or press Enter or Space, to hide or show a series; the
  axis and any stacked total follow. A hidden item is struck through and its
  swatch is hollow. At least one series always stays visible. Hovering or
  focusing an item highlights that series in the plot.

## Compact numbers

Axis ticks and the legend's Mean and Max use a compact form when you do not pass a
formatter: `536k`, `2.3M`, `1.2B`, and at most three significant digits below a
thousand. A long decimal such as 536,440.143 is never printed. When you pass
`formatValue`, it is used instead (currency, percent, durations), and a computed
figure such as a mean is rounded tidily before it reaches your function.

## Colour: six at most

The chart tokens give six categorical colours, `chart-1` to `chart-6`, plus the
neutral `chart-mid`. They are validated to be told apart in every theme, but no
chart needs more than six, and more than a handful is hard to read. So every
chart that takes many series folds the surplus into one **Other** series drawn
in `chart-mid`:

- **LineChart** with more than six series keeps the five with the highest
  maximum value (in their original order) and sums the rest, point by point,
  into Other. Six or fewer are drawn as given. Pass `colorIndex` on a series to
  keep its colour steady when the ranking changes between loads.
- **DonutChart** keeps the first five slices in the order you pass and sums the
  rest into Other. Sort largest first to keep the largest five.
- **GroupedBarChart** keeps the first three bars of each group (`chart-1` to
  `chart-3`) and sums the rest into Other.

The Other series is always a sum. That is right for counts, tokens and spend. For
a rate or an average, merge the series yourself before you pass them in. The
visually hidden data table of each chart still lists every series you passed, so
nothing is lost to a screen reader.

## Never colour alone

Colour is never the only cue:

- The legend names every series, the tooltip lists name and value beside each
  swatch, and the donut legend lists the label, value and share of every slice in
  text. Every chart has a readout line that names a position and its values in
  words.
- In the normal themes lines are plain and bars and slices are solid; there is no
  hatching and no marker on every point.
- In **high contrast** the charts add what colour alone cannot say. When the
  `hc` theme is on, or the system asks for `prefers-contrast: more` or forced
  colours, bars and slices gain ECharts decal patterns (stripes, dots and so on),
  and lines show a marker at every point (circle, square, triangle, diamond) with
  the second four dashed.

## Keyboard and screen readers

- The drawing is hidden from assistive technology. A visually hidden table,
  captioned with the chart `title`, carries every data point.
- The chart is one tab stop. Focus it and the readout line shows the first
  position, with the tooltip and highlight on the plot; the arrow keys, Home and
  End move along the x steps, groups or slices, and Escape clears it. Hovering a
  position shows the tooltip.
- The readout is a polite live region while the chart has focus, so each
  position is read out as you move.
- Give every chart a `title`. It names the group and captions the hidden table.

## Empty, gaps and narrow containers

- No points, or no series, shows a short empty state instead of a drawing. Set
  its words with `emptyText`.
- A `null` value is a gap in a line or a skipped bar. It is never drawn as zero,
  and the hidden table says "No data".
- The drawing follows its container with a resize observer and keeps its text at
  a fixed size. In a narrow container overlapping x labels are dropped and long
  legends wrap. `width` sets only the starting width while the container has no
  measurable width (a hidden tab, a server render).
- Charts draw in with a short animation. It is off when the system asks for
  reduced motion or the app sets `reducedMotion="reduce"`.

## Values and formatting

Each chart takes a `formatValue` (the combo takes `formatBar` and `formatLine`)
for axis ticks, the tooltip, the legend and the readout. A point's own `display`
wins over the formatter for that point. Axes use 4 or 5 round ticks (steps of 1,
2, 2.5 or 5 times a power of ten) and always include zero.
